# Hypertension Management Guidelines

## What is Hypertension?
Hypertension, commonly known as high blood pressure, is a chronic medical condition in which the
blood pressure in the arteries is persistently elevated. Blood pressure is recorded as two numbers:
systolic pressure (the pressure during heartbeats) over diastolic pressure (the pressure between
beats). A normal reading is around 120/80 mmHg.

## Classification of Blood Pressure
- Normal: systolic below 120 and diastolic below 80.
- Elevated: systolic 120-129 and diastolic below 80.
- Stage 1 Hypertension: systolic 130-139 or diastolic 80-89.
- Stage 2 Hypertension: systolic 140 or higher or diastolic 90 or higher.
- Hypertensive Crisis: systolic over 180 and/or diastolic over 120, requiring immediate medical care.

## Causes and Risk Factors
Primary hypertension has no identifiable cause and develops gradually over many years. Secondary
hypertension is caused by an underlying condition such as kidney disease, thyroid problems, sleep
apnea, or certain medications. Major risk factors include age, family history, obesity, physical
inactivity, tobacco use, excessive salt intake, low potassium, excessive alcohol, and chronic stress.

## Management and Treatment
Lifestyle modifications are the first line of treatment: reducing dietary sodium to under 2,300 mg
per day, following the DASH diet, regular aerobic exercise (at least 150 minutes weekly), weight
loss, limiting alcohol, and stress management. When lifestyle changes are insufficient, medication
is prescribed. Common classes include ACE inhibitors, ARBs, calcium channel blockers, diuretics,
and beta-blockers. Treatment targets generally aim for blood pressure below 130/80 mmHg in most
adults.

## Monitoring
Home blood pressure monitoring is encouraged, with readings taken at the same time each day,
sitting quietly for five minutes first. Keep a log and share it with your clinician. Persistent
high readings should trigger a medication review rather than self-adjustment.
