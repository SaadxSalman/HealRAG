# Healthy Sleep and Insomnia

## Why Sleep Matters
Sleep is essential for physical and mental health. Adults generally need 7 to 9 hours of quality
sleep per night. Chronic sleep deprivation is linked to obesity, heart disease, weakened immunity,
impaired cognition, mood disorders, and reduced productivity.

## Common Sleep Disorders
Insomnia is the most common sleep disorder, characterized by difficulty falling asleep, staying
asleep, or waking too early. Other disorders include obstructive sleep apnea, restless legs
syndrome, narcolepsy, and circadian rhythm disorders such as shift work disorder.

## Sleep Hygiene Tips
- Maintain a consistent sleep-wake schedule, even on weekends.
- Keep the bedroom cool, dark, and quiet.
- Avoid caffeine and nicotine in the late afternoon and evening.
- Limit alcohol, which disrupts sleep architecture.
- Avoid screens (blue light) 30-60 minutes before bed.
- Establish a relaxing pre-sleep routine such as reading or light stretching.
- Get natural light exposure in the morning.

## When to Seek Help
If poor sleep persists for more than three nights per week for three months, or if daytime
fatigue interferes with daily life, consult a clinician. Cognitive behavioral therapy for
insomnia (CBT-I) is the first-line recommended treatment and is more effective long-term than
sleeping pills.
