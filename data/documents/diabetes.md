# Type 2 Diabetes: A Practical Overview

## What Is Type 2 Diabetes?
Type 2 diabetes is a chronic condition that affects the way the body metabolizes glucose (sugar).
In this condition, the body either resists the effects of insulin or does not produce enough
insulin to maintain a normal glucose level. It is the most common form of diabetes and typically
develops in adults, though it is increasingly seen in younger people.

## Key Symptoms
Symptoms often develop slowly and may include increased thirst, frequent urination, extreme
hunger, unintended weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing sores, and frequent
infections. Many people have no symptoms for years.

## Diagnosis
Diagnosis is confirmed through blood tests: a fasting plasma glucose of 126 mg/dL or higher,
an HbA1c of 6.5% or higher, a random plasma glucose of 200 mg/dL or higher with symptoms, or a
two-hour oral glucose tolerance test value of 200 mg/dL or higher.

## Management
Management focuses on blood glucose control to prevent complications. This includes a balanced
diet rich in fiber and whole grains, regular physical activity, weight management, glucose
monitoring, and oral medications such as metformin. Some people require insulin therapy.
The American Diabetes Association generally recommends an HbA1c target of below 7% for most
nonpregnant adults.

## Complications
Uncontrolled diabetes can lead to cardiovascular disease, nerve damage (neuropathy), kidney
damage (nephropathy), eye damage (retinopathy), foot damage, skin conditions, hearing impairment,
and Alzheimer's disease risk.
